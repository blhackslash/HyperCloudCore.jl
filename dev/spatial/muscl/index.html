MUSCL Reconstruction and Limiting

MUSCL reconstructs the solution around every particle with an MLS Taylor polynomial, evaluates both sides' polynomials at the midpoint of each particle pair, resolves the jump with a numerical flux, and differentiates the resulting interface fluxes. It is the scheme with the most knobs: arbitrary order, a priori slope limiting, and the Edge Polynomial Degree (EPD) strategies for MOOD.

From finite volumes to particles

In finite-volume MUSCL each cell carries a polynomial that is evaluated at the cell faces. On a particle cloud:

  • The "cell" is a particle and its polynomial is an MLS fit of state differences over its neighbourhood (the cached gradients).
  • The "face" between $i$ and $j$ is the midpoint $x_i + x_{ij}/2$. The two reconstructed states are

\[U_{ij} = U_i + \sum_{\beta} c_{i,\beta}\, p_\beta\!\left(\tfrac12 x_{ij}\right), \qquad U_{ji} = U_j + \sum_{\beta} c_{j,\beta}\, p_\beta\!\left(-\tfrac12 x_{ij}\right).\]

  • The divergence is $2\,\mathcal D_i\big[F_{\text{num}}(U_{ij}, U_{ji}) - F(U_i) + \text{nc\_jump}\big]$.

Construction

MUSCL(T, D, M, order, limiter, flux; div_order = 0)
  • order ≥ 1 is the consistency order. The reconstruction has degree order - 1: order = 1 is piecewise constant (first-order upwind with the given flux), order = 2 linear, order = 3 quadratic, and so on.
  • limiter: NoLimiter(), BarthJespersenLimiter(mode), VenkatakrishnanLimiter(mode), and (1-D only) MinmodLimiter(mode), SuperbeeLimiter(mode). mode = :hard limits all coefficients, :soft only the first-order ones.
  • flux: a NumericalFluxFunction.
  • div_order: with 0 the divergence fit follows the particle's current order $k_i$; with a positive value it uses div_order for every particle, independent of order drops (keeps the divergence weights of neighbouring particles consistent when MOOD lowers orders). The degree that order maps to is described in Divergence degree.
  • The divergence fit has a constant term and runs over the neighbours only, so its weights sum to zero; see Divergence fits: weights that sum to zero.

Divergence degree

For a divergence order $o$ (the particle's order, or div_order) the divergence is fitted with degree

order $o$123456…
reconstruction degree012345$o - 1$
divergence degree113355$o - 1$ (even $o$), $o$ (odd $o$)

Why odd orders go one degree up. On a (nearly) symmetric stencil, the first derivative of a least-squares fit of degree $q$ is accurate to $O(h^q)$ for even $q$ but to $O(h^{q+1})$ for odd $q$: the first monomial the fit misses, $x^{q+1}$, is odd for even $q$ and aliases into the gradient (it projects onto $x$), while for odd $q$ it is even and orthogonal to the gradient. Degree $o - 1$ is therefore enough for even orders, but MUSCL3 and MUSCL5 were only second and fourth order with it. Fitting the divergence of an odd order with degree $o$ restores the order (smooth advection on 20 % jittered grids: MUSCL3 EOC 2.0 → 3.0, errors 7× smaller). It does not depend on the reconstruction degree: reconstruction degree 2 with divergence degree 2 is second order, with divergence degree 3 third order.

The higher degree has two conditions, checked per particle (_divergence_index):

  • the stencil has at least 1.5× as many neighbours as the fit has unknowns ($B(D, o) + 1$, with the constant term). An exactly determined fit is fragile on moving grids; in 1-D, MUSCL3 needs $R \ge 3.5\Delta x$ (6 neighbours for 4 unknowns), otherwise it keeps degree 2 and second order;
  • in 3-D only up to degree 3 (basis size).

Independently, every divergence fit lowers its degree where a particle has fewer neighbours than unknowns.

Supported orders

The fits are solved inside the kernels with StaticArrays matrices. Up to 24 unknowns the Cholesky factorisation is unrolled and runs on every backend; larger fits fall back to a heap-allocating LAPACK call, which still works on the CPU backends (slower) but does not compile for GPUs. The largest fit of a scheme is the reconstruction ($B(D, o-1)$ unknowns) or the divergence ($B(D, q) + 1$):

(Verified with a few steps of 2-D MUSCL7 and 3-D MUSCL5 on KABackend(ROCBackend()), which fail to compile, and on PolyesterBackend(), which run.) Two more limits apply to high orders: the CFL estimate evaluates the derivative stencil with at most degree 4 (degree 3 in 3-D), so for higher divergence degrees the time step is estimated from the degree-4 stencil and slightly too large; and the radius must provide enough neighbours for the reconstruction, $B(D, o - 1)$, or particles drop to first order (see the pre-gather pass below).

The two passes

  1. Pre-gather (update_content!). Fits the reconstruction at the particle's current order and stores the (limited) coefficients in gradients[i]. If the neighbourhood has fewer than $B(D, k-1)$ neighbours, the particle's order is set to 1 in particle_orders, so MOOD and the EPD rules see the starved particle as first order.
  2. Flux. For every neighbour, it chooses the interface degrees from the MOOD strategy, masks both reconstructions to those degrees (mask_basis), evaluates the numerical flux and non-conservative jump, and differentiates the interface differences.

Because the flux at $i$ reads gradients[j] of its neighbours, all gradients of a stage must be computed before any flux; that is why the pre-gather pass is separate.

Slope limiting (a priori)

  • Barth–Jespersen / Venkatakrishnan (any dimension). The reconstruction is evaluated at every interface. If an interface value leaves the range $[\min_{\nu(i)} U, \max_{\nu(i)} U]$, the coefficients are scaled by $\phi = \min_j \phi(r_j)$ with $\phi_{BJ}(r) = \min(1, r)$ and the smooth Venkatakrishnan function. This is done per state component.
  • Minmod / Superbee (1-D only). They compare the left and right one-sided slopes from the nearest neighbours on each side.

Limiting and MOOD can be combined: the limiter acts on every reconstruction, and MOOD reduces the order where the limited update is still inadmissible.

Interface degrees and EPD strategies

When MOOD lowers some particles' orders, the two sides of an edge can have different degrees. The MOODStrategy decides which degrees are used at the interface, via _get_interface_orders(strategy, pg, i, j):

StrategyInterface degrees $(d_{i\to j}, d_{j\to i})$Neighbour forcing
EPD0$(k_i, k_j)$: each side uses its own order (mismatched reconstruction allowed)none
StrictEPD0$(k_i, k_j)$when $i$ drops, its neighbours are lowered to $k_i$
EPD1$(m, m)$ with $m = \min(k_i, k_j)$none
EPD2$(m, m)$ with $m = \min_{l \in \nu(i)\cup\nu(j)\cup\{i,j\}} k_l$none

(Interface degree $k$ means a degree-$(k-1)$ polynomial.) These map the finite-volume EPD ideas onto edges of the neighbour graph. Moving down the table, the transition between high and low order becomes wider and smoother, and more particles have to be recomputed after a drop: an order change at $p$ alters fluxes up to _order_reach(::MUSCL, strategy) hops away, which is 1 for EPD0, StrictEPD0 and EPD1, and 2 for EPD2. The stepper uses this to limit recomputation to the affected particles (see Multi-Dimensional Optimal Order Detection (MOOD)).

Stepper hooks

The end of MUSCL.jl defines the hooks that tell the time stepper and MOOD about MUSCL:

_order_reach(::MUSCL, ::MOODStrategy) = 1     # 2 for EPD2
_divergence_degree(::MUSCL)                     # degree of the full-order divergence fit, for the CFL estimate
has_cached_gradients(::MUSCL) = true          # enables MOODu2's curvature test
cached_gradients(m::MUSCL) = m.gradients

They have to come after the MUSCL type definition. The time-stepping files never name MUSCL themselves.

A divergence interpolator executing MUSCL-type interface reconstruction and flux evaluation. It dynamically tracks particle polynomial limits strictly via the underlying grid core.

Constructors

MUSCL(::Type{T}, dimension::Int, M::Int, max_order::Int, limiter, flux; div_order::Int=0)
  • T: The numeric type (e.g., Float64).
  • dimension: Spatial dimension.
  • M: Number of state components.
  • max_order: Maximum consistency order; the reconstruction has degree `max_order - 1.
  • limiter: The slope/gradient limiter applied during the pre-gather pass to enforce monotonicity.
  • flux: The numerical flux function used to resolve interface states.
  • div_order: Target order for the final divergence calculation (defaults to 0, adapting to the maximum available order). The divergence is fitted with degree order - 1 for even and degree order for odd orders (see _div_idx), with a constant term so that its weights sum to zero.

Callable / Functor

(muscl::MUSCL)(eq, i, f_i, nb_slice, pg, ib, strat) -> State{M, T}

Computes the divergence of the flux at particle i. Note that MUSCL relies on a pre-calculation pass (update_content!) to compute and limit the local gradients beforehand. The execution follows these steps:

  • Retrieves the pre-calculated, limited gradients for particle i and its neighbors.
  • Evaluates the reconstructed states at the midpoint between particles using distance-scaled spatial basis polynomials.
  • Interface degrees are chosen by the MOOD strategy via _get_interface_orders (EPD0/StrictEPD0: own order per side; EPD1: edge minimum; EPD2: neighbourhood minimum).
  • Resolves the interface using the provided numerical flux function and evaluates non-conservative jumps.
  • Computes the final divergence using the dynamically resolved divergence order.

Fields

  • interpolators: Pre-allocated tuple of interpolators up to MAX_ORDER, using ConstantReconstruction for the first-order base.
  • limiter: The assigned slope limiter.
  • flux: The associated numerical flux function (NF).
  • gradients: A pre-allocated vector storing the reconstructed, limited spatial gradients for all particles.
  • grad_weights, div_weights: cached inverse MLS moment matrices (MLSWeights) of the full-order reconstruction and divergence fits, refreshed by prepare_geometry! when the grid geometry changes.

The per-particle caches are Growable buffers whose array type is the storage keyword of the constructor (Array by default; ROCArray etc. for a grid built with device_grid).

Slope limiters used to enforce monotonicity during MUSCL interface reconstruction.

  • BarthJespersenLimiter: Applies a standard strict minimum/maximum bounding.
  • VenkatakrishnanLimiter: Applies a differentiable, smooth relaxation of the Barth-Jespersen limiter bounds.
  • SuperbeeLimiter: A 1D-focused limiter applying the Superbee bounding function.
  • MinmodLimiter: A 1D-focused limiter returning the minimum modulus of the local slopes.
  • All limiters can be instantiated with a specific Mode (such as :soft or :hard).

HyperCloudCore._limit_slopes — Method
_limit_slopes(strategy, raw_grad, nb_slice, f_i, f_neighbors, pg, distVec, ::Val{DEGREE})

Applies the specified slope limiter strategy to the raw gradient of a target particle.

Details

  • For BarthJespersen and Venkatakrishnan, it evaluates local maxima and minima, reconstructs the interface state using the raw gradient, and computes a limiting coefficient phi for each target equation.
  • For Minmod and Superbee, it computes left and right finite difference slopes based on neighbor distances and limits the gradient strictly along a 1D stencil.
  • Returns a limited gradient vector where specific polynomial basis terms are scaled by the bounding coefficient phi.
