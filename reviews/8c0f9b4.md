### Findings

#### N1. Ghost band thinner than the support radius on axes with a smaller spacing — low/medium — CONFIRMED
`src/Grid/Domains.jl:159-162` (`ComputationalDomain`: `N_ghost = ceil(Int, interp_range_factor)` spacings of
`dxs_f[d]` per axis, with `interp_range_factor = R / max_dx`), documented the same way in
`docs/src/particle_grids/domains.md` ("extended by ⌈R/Δx_max⌉ spacings").
On an axis whose spacing is below `max_dx` the band is `ceil(R/max_dx)·dx[d] < R` thick, so interior particles
within `R` of that wall miss the ghost particles between the band edge and `R`.
- Scenario: `build_particle_grid` on `[0,2]×[0,1]` with `Ns = (100, 100)` gives `dx = (0.02, 0.01)`, `R = 2.5·0.02 = 0.05`:
  the band reaches `x = −0.06` on the left but only `y = −0.03` at the bottom. A wall particle at the bottom has 34
  neighbours, one at the left wall 37, a centre particle 37. With `dx = (0.1, 0.05)`: 38 at the bottom vs 42.
- Effect: one-sided, truncated stencils and a weaker ghost (boundary-condition) influence on the walls of the
  finer axis; the treatment of the two walls differs. Consistency of the MLS fits is not lost (impact on accuracy
  not measured, hence low/medium).
- Evidence: `probe_aniso_band.jl` (4 failures; numbers above).
- Fix: per-axis layers `ceil(Int, R / dx[d])` (i.e. `interp_range_factor * max_dx / dx[d]`); the narrow-band
  cutoff already uses `R + max_dx`. Add the probe as a regression test.

#### N2. Adaptivity events next to a wall rewrite the ghost particles' states (incl. Dirichlet data) — medium — CONFIRMED
`src/Grid/Adaptivity.jl:370-376` (local set = every particle within `R(1+2√D)+dx`, ghosts included), `:451-458`
(swept contributions `δm` for every local particle), `:489-494` (residual correction), `:496-528` (DMP repair and
write-back of every survivor of the local set).
Events only start at interior particles, but their remap treats ghosts like any other particle: their shape
functions change near the event, so they receive `δV`, `δm`, a share of the residual and of the DMP repair, and
`U`/`V` of the ghosts are overwritten.
- Scenario (`probe_adapt_ghosts.jl`): 1-D `[0,1]`, `dx = 0.05`, `bc_map = Dict(1 => FixedDirichlet())`, `U = 1 + x`,
  the particle at 0.05 moved to 0.015 → one merge with the wall particle at 0. After `adapt_particles!` the ghost
  states are `0.85 → 0.85060`, `0.90 → 0.90070`, `0.95 → 0.95290` (6 % of the local variation at `x = −0.05`).
  `FixedDirichlet` restores ghosts to `pg.rhos`, which now holds the remapped values: the **Dirichlet data are changed
  permanently**. With `OutflowBC` the ghost share of the remap is overwritten at the next boundary-condition pass,
  so the "exactly conservative" `mass_residual ≈ 0` reported by the call does not survive the next stage.
- Not caught by the tests: all adaptivity tests use periodic grids or uniform states near walls (the new
  "no insertion next to a ghost" test uses a constant state).
- Fix options: exclude events whose local set contains boundary particles (like the shock band, cheap: mark
  `touched` around every boundary particle within `R(1+2√D)+dx`); or keep ghosts out of `aff` and put their swept
  content on the interior survivors (keeps Σ V U of the interior exact, Dirichlet data untouched).

#### N3. `adapt_particles!` can still throw `CapacityError` after changing the grid (slot reservation) — low — CONFIRMED
`src/Grid/Adaptivity.jl:100-106` (the new pre-check covers the per-particle buffers only) and `:121-123`
(`_apply_topology!`, then `pg.neighbor(pg)`, whose `ensure_capacity!(nd, capacity)` at `NeighborLogic.jl:263` can throw).
The docstring (`:79-80`) now says "On a grid whose buffers `reserve!` froze, a `CapacityError` is thrown before the
grid is changed". That holds for the particle capacity, not for the neighbour slots: an insertion adds a particle
*and* neighbour slots on both sides, and the slot reservation is `particles × slots_per_particle`.
- Scenario (`probe_reserve_adapt.jl`): 1-D periodic, 50 particles, one gap of 1.7 dx, `reserve!` with the default
  `slots_per_particle = slots_per_particle(pg)` (3.92) and room for 51 particles (200 slots). `adapt_particles!` inserts
  one particle, then the neighbour search needs 204 slots → `CapacityError(Int64, 200, 204)` with `pg.meta.N = 51`
  already, and `ranges` pointing up to slot 204 of 200-element slot arrays (inconsistent neighbour structure).
  The state itself is the conservative post-remap state, so a run that stops is fine; a caller that catches the
  error and continues gets out-of-range slot ranges.
- Fix: before the first remap, also bound the slot need (e.g. `capacity ≥ current + 2·Σ_events (added) · max_nb`
  on row-major, `N_new · (max_nb + 1)` on slot-major) against `length(nd.indices)` when the slot arrays are fixed,
  or soften the docstring to "per-particle buffers" and recommend headroom in `slots_per_particle` (the `reserve!`
  docstring already says to raise it for adaptive runs).

#### N4. Wall snapping misses in Float32 on fine 1-D grids — low — CONFIRMED
`src/Grid/Domains.jl:214-221`. The snap tolerance is `sqrt(eps(T))·dx`, but the round-off of
`canvas_mins + (I−1)·dx` grows like `eps(T)·|x|` plus the accumulated error of `dx` itself; in Float32 it exceeds
the tolerance once `|x|/dx` is a few thousand.
- Scenario: Float32 `[0, 1]`, `Ns = 3900, 4000, 5900, …` (47 of `Ns = 100:100:20000`), `[-1, 1]` 80 of 200: a wall
  lattice point is again classified as a ghost (the asymmetry of round-1 finding 1). Float64 is fine for any
  realistic resolution.
- Evidence: `probe_snap_float32.jl`.
- Fix: generate non-periodic coordinates from the wall (`geom.mins[d] + (I[d] − 1 − N_ghost)·dx`) and snap with a
  tolerance that includes `|x|` (e.g. `sqrt(eps(T))·dx + 8eps(T)·max(abs(mins), abs(maxs))`), or compute the
  wall index `k = round(Int, (maxs − mins)/dx)` and set that point exactly.

#### N5. `device_grid(Array, host)` docstring: arrays stay shared after growth — low (docs) — CONFIRMED
`src/DeviceAdapt.jl:66-71` (new text of finding 13): "writes through either grid are seen by both until one of them
grows a buffer". Host `Growable`s grow with `resize!` in place (`Growable.jl:89`), so both wrappers keep the same
`Vector` and stay aliased after growth (`probe_device_grid_alias.jl`: after `ensure_capacity!(dg, 4N)` the write
through `dg` is visible in `pg`, same object). What does diverge is the metadata (`N`, `max_nb`, …) and, on
non-`Vector` storage, the arrays. Suggest: "they stay shared (host growth is in place); only the metadata is
independent".

Extra fix reviewed — **abea787 (KABackend work-group fallback): OK.** In KA 0.10.1 every backend launch goes
through `KI.check_work_group_size` *before* `KI.launch` (`backend_launch.jl`), so the first failing launch has no side
effects, and the matched message is exactly KernelInterface's `throw_work_group_size_error` /
`throw_work_group_dims_error` ("Work-group size …"); other errors are rethrown. The cache can only switch a kernel
to KA's own choice, which is always valid, so a stale or shared key (e.g. two GPUs behind the same backend object)
costs at most occupancy, never correctness. Thread safety: insertions and lookups are under the lock; the
`isempty` pre-check reads the `Set` unlocked (formally a data race with a concurrent `push!`, benign in practice);
4 concurrent first launches gave one entry and correct results (`probe_ka_tuned.jl`). Hot path: one lock + hash per
launch once the set is non-empty (≈ 80 ns, no extra allocation measured). On real GPUs the fallback only triggers
if the vendor backend reports a per-kernel limit below the configured size (CUDA: register-limited `maxthreads`);
behaviour on AMDGPU unchanged otherwise. Fragility: matching on the message text silently disables the fallback (the
original error then surfaces) if KernelInterface rewords it. The regression test exercises the real path (a
4096-element dynamically indexed `SVector` forces PoCL below 256) and checks the cache entry.

### Findings

#### N1 (low, CONFIRMED) — Venkatakrishnan limiter returns NaN in Float32 for large ratios
`src/Interpolation/Limiter.jl:37-40` (`(r² + 2r)/(r² + r + 2)`), reached from `_update_phi` (`Limiter.jl:68-79`).
- `r² ` overflows Float32 for `r ≳ 1.8e19`, giving `Inf/Inf = NaN`, and `min(phi, NaN) = NaN` (Base `min`, now also
  `math_min`) makes the whole limited gradient NaN. `r = (u_max − f_i)/recon` with `|recon| > 1e-12` (absolute
  threshold) reaches that when a component's local range exceeds ~1.8e7 and an interface increment is just above
  1e-12 (e.g. Float32 Euler/BGK in SI units at 100 bar, energies ~1e7, in a near-flat region).
- Evidence: `probe_misc.jl`: `limiter_phi(VenkatakrishnanLimiter(), 1f20) = NaN` (Float64: 1.0);
  `_update_phi(VK, 1, 1f-11, 0, 1f9, -1f9) = NaN`.
- Fix: evaluate as `(r + 2)/(r + 1 + 2/r)` for `r ≥ 1`, or clamp `r_pos` to e.g. `T(1e6)` (φ is 1 − O(1/r)).
  Pre-existing (the F8 change did not cause it); the F7 relative-threshold fix would also make it unreachable.

#### N2 (low, CONFIRMED by inspection) — docs imply the SSP IMEX pairs get the substep MOOD guarantee
`docs/src/time_integration/tableaus.md:27` says the stage candidates are checked "for the non-SSP tableaus (RK4, the
ARS pairs)", and the table marks `IMEX_Euler`, `IMEX_SSP2332`, `IMEX_PRSSP3` "Explicit part SSP: yes". But
`ssp_coefficient` returns 0 for every tableau with a non-zero implicit part (`ButcherTableaus.jl:84`), so these
pairs also only get the stage-candidate check (`substep_ratio = 0`). The `IMEXButcherTableau` docstring
(`ButcherTableaus.jl:20`, "the IMEX tableaus") is right; the page is not. Fix: "(RK4 and every IMEX pair)".

#### N3 (low, CONFIRMED) — the stepper-level IMEX order test cannot see coupling errors
`test/unit_schemes.jl:290-310` ("IMEX tableaus: observed order through the stepper"). Advection on a periodic
lattice and a constant-rate decay `−λu` commute, so the step is a function of `(z_E, z_I)` whose `z_E z_I` term only
depends on the *sum* `b̃·c + b·c̃`. A pair that violates both coupling conditions but has the right sum passes.
- Evidence: `probe_imex_commuting.jl`: the 2-stage pair `A = [0.2 0; 1 0.2]`, `b = (0.7, 0.3)`, Heun explicit part
  (`b̃·c = 0.7`, `b·c̃ = 0.3`) gives EOC 2.02 in the test's own setup, EOC 1.0 on `u' = −u cos t − λ(u − sin t)`.
- Impact: low, because the algebraic test in `unit_core.jl:502-523` checks every coupling condition. Make the
  stepper test non-commuting (e.g. a decay rate `λ(x)` that varies in space, or a time-dependent target
  `−λ(u − g(t))`), so it is an independent check.
